# AI Use Reflection

I used ChatGPT (OpenAI) for technical and substantive support. Technically, it helped me interpret the brief, organise headings, format the comparison table in Markdown, check word counts and plan meaningful Git commits. Substantively, I supplied the lecture slides and notes, then used ChatGPT to suggest a policy client, compare options, draft wording and identify sources. I retained DEWR as the client and the employer-supported training pilot because the approach directly connected task displacement, augmentation and worker adjustment.

I rejected the initial framing that relied only on DEWR's July 2026 monitoring report. Although current, it was a report rather than the clearest policy process. I asked for a stronger anchor and used the government's March 2026 response to the *Future of Work* inquiry. I also substantially changed an early $120 million cost suggestion because it lacked a transparent calculation. The final $115 million estimate itemises worker subsidies, administration and evaluation and labels the figures as proposed costs.

The main limitation was that AI could present assumptions and recent claims confidently before confirming them. I therefore used direct Australian government documents and original research in the reference list, kept uncertainty in DEWR's findings, and avoided treating occupational exposure as proven job loss.
