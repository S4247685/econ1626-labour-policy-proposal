# AI Use Reflection

I used ChatGPT for technical and drafting assistance, not as a substitute for my research or judgement. It helped me interpret the instructions, plan the brief's structure, improve clarity, format Markdown tables and links, check the word limit, and organise meaningful Git commits. These uses developed my technical skills by demonstrating how a policy repository should be structured and validated.

For research and argument-building, I began with the lecture concepts and checked claims against original sources from DEWR, Jobs and Skills Australia, the OECD and peer-reviewed studies. AI helped identify search terms and sources, but I did not treat its summaries as evidence. I read the material, selected the evidence, compared the options and selected preventative employer-supported training as the strongest recommendation.

I rejected an initial suggestion to anchor the proposal in DEWR's July 2026 monitoring report. I replaced it with the Australian Government's March 2026 response to the *Future of Work* inquiry because it represented a clearer policy process. I also edited the proposed cost into a transparent $115 million calculation.

A limitation was AI's tendency to present assumptions confidently because it predicts language rather than verifies facts. I therefore checked dates, titles, calculations and references before accepting them.
